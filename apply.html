How to Apply

New this round: the Fellowship application will consist of two steps, outlined below. You’ll apply online via Submittable, but before you start, we encourage you to read the full guidelines carefully. A little time up front makes for a smoother process and a stronger application.

Apply in Submittable

Step 1: Step1Work Samples & CV

Due Jan 28, 2027

1. Eligibility Questionnaire

Complete the eligibility questionnaire in Submittable. The questionnaire lets you know if you meet the fundamental eligibility requirements. If you are eligible, you will be directed to Step 1 of the application. If you are not eligible, you will receive an automatic notification.

2. Artist CV

A detailed Artist CV is required (not a one-page resume).

Your CV must include:

  • Your generative work
  • Education credits and dates
  • All artistic recognition to date

3. Work Samples

Required work samples must be:

  • Created by the applicant/s. You must be the generative artist for every sample and hold the rights to the work. Co-applicants must submit work generated by both co-applicants and only the two co-applicants.
  • Created within the last 10 years (2017–2027)
  • Created outside of any time spent in a degree program
  • Publicly presented (i.e., works that have been shared in an exhibition, presentation, screening, reading, publication, or other public setting)

Work Sample Requirements by Artistic Field

On February 25, if your application is eligible, you will be invited to complete Step 2. If your application does not meet eligibility requirements, you will receive a notification.

Step 2: Step2Core Questions

Due Apr 7, 2027

Artists whose applications are determined to be eligible in Step 1 will be invited to submit Step 2: Core Questions. The Core Questions correspond directly to the review criteria of Creative Risk, Creative Vision, and Creative Engagement and Impact.

You may choose to respond in writing or on video, whichever you feel allows you to make the strongest and most authentic case for your application. Financial need is not a criterion. This is not a project grant.

Creative Risk

  • Describe a specific artistic risk you are taking in your work—something that is untested, that departs from your past practice, or that pushes against convention in your field. Why is it timely and relevant to take that risk now?

Creative Vision

  • Name three values that drive your work. List three words, no further explanation required.
  • What are the central questions or concerns driving your work, and how might you explore these further during the Fellowship?
  • In thinking about artistic lineage, who are you in conversation with as you make your work?
  • How does the content of your work connect to your own identity and history?

Creative Engagement and Impact

  • Describe a specific encounter between your work and a community member, audience member, or participant you seek to engage. What happened, and what did it teach you?
  • Name three goals for this Fellowship. What impact do you hope the Fellowship will have on you and your work?
  • As an early career artist, share why this is a good time in your practice for a three-year period of focused development.

You will be notified of your application status no later than December 18, 2027.

How Applications Are Reviewed

  1. Staff eligibility review

    Jerome Foundation staff review every Step 1 application to confirm your eligibility as an early career artist in your selected artistic field and that your work samples are eligible. See the full guidelines for detailed information on early career stage and work sample eligibility requirements.

  2. Panel review

    Applications that advance to Step 2 will be reviewed and assessed by diverse, field-specific panels based on the review criteria of Creative Vision, Creative Risk, and Creative Engagement and Impact.

  3. Finalist review and Fellowship recommendations

    The panel identifies a group of finalists whose applications are reviewed collectively in a day-long panel meeting.

  4. Board approval

    Panels recommend Fellows and alternates to the Jerome Foundation Board of Directors, which reviews and authorizes the recommendations and awards the Fellowships.
